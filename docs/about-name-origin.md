# Origins of the name Governor

In 1788 James Watt bolted a pair of iron balls to a spinning spindle and connected it to the throttle of his steam engine. When the engine ran fast, the spindle spun faster, the balls flew outward, and the linkage they pulled closed the throttle a little. When the engine slowed, the balls dropped and the throttle opened. Nobody had to stand beside the machine with a hand on the valve: the engine watched its own output and corrected itself.

That device, the centrifugal or flyball governor, is the symbol on our logo. It is one of the first widely used feedback controllers, and in 1868 James Clerk Maxwell's paper *On Governors* used it to found the mathematical study of control systems. Eighty years later Norbert Wiener named the field of *cybernetics* after the Greek *kybernētēs*, the steersman — the same root that gives English the word "governor".

The fit with this project is deliberate. Governor is for building AI agents and the interfaces around them, and the hard part of agents is rarely raw power. It is regulation: keeping a capable, fast-moving system pointed where you want it, observing what it actually did, and feeding that back into the next run. Tools, traces, memory, review, and the people who compose agents in the web UI are our flyballs and linkage.

Governor began life as [Gofannon](https://github.com/apache/tooling-gofannon), named for the Welsh god of smithcraft who forged tools for heroes. The forge made the tools; the governor keeps the engine they drive running true.
